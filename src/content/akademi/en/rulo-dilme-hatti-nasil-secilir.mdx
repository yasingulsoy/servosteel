A coil slitting line is an investment that splits a wide steel coil into strips of the required width — and if it is not sized correctly, you either run out of capacity or overpay for a machine you do not need. The 7 criteria below summarise what to clarify before requesting a quote.

## 1. Coil capacity and weight

The heart of the line is the **maximum coil weight** you will process. Light series run 1,500–4,000 kg, heavy series 6,000–15,000 kg. Size for your volume **2–3 years from now**, not today — the frame cannot be enlarged later.

## 2. Sheet thickness and width

Slitting force depends directly on sheet thickness. Thin sheet (0.5–1 mm) and thick sheet (1.0–5 mm) require completely different knife sets and frame rigidity. The maximum **strip width** must also match your coil width.

## 3. The slitting (knife) unit

This is what determines strip quality. The knife clearance must be **precisely adjustable** to sheet thickness for a burr-free edge. If you change dimensions often, a quick knife-change system saves serious time.

## 4. Straightening requirement

Coil-set memory causes flatness problems in the final product. If your customers demand tight flatness, a **straightening unit** is added to the line; planning it up front is cheaper than modifying the line later.

## 5. Recoiler and tension

As slit strips are re-coiled, **tension control** becomes critical; loose winding causes telescoping during transport and downstream processing. Separator discs and the tension system must be sized to your strip count.

## 6. Automation and control

Single-operator management, speed control and recipe memory drive efficiency. On Servosteel lines the control architecture is built on **Schneider Lexium and Altivar** drives, with the whole line managed from one panel.

## 7. Manufacturer and service

The most critical yet most overlooked point. A line built by a **single manufacturer** (from decoiler to recoiler) eliminates integration problems from the start. Pin down commissioning, spare parts and remote support in the contract.
