In a progressive die the part is not finished at a single station: with every press stroke the strip advances one station and is punched, cut and formed in sequence. That is why the performance of the die is often decided not by the die itself but by **the feeder that advances the strip.** If the pitch drifts by a few hundredths of a millimetre, the punch moves off centre, burrs appear and — in the worst case — the die is damaged.

The 6 criteria below summarise what to clarify when selecting a feeder for your progressive die.

## 1. Pitch accuracy — the critical number

A progressive die does not forgive accumulated error: a small deviation repeated at every station becomes a total shift at the last one. So the first figure to look at is **feed accuracy.**

On Servosteel servo feeders, feed accuracy is **± 0.1 mm**. When you calculate your die's tolerance budget, weigh this value together with the number of stations.

## 2. Pitch length and station count

The die's station spacing (pitch) is the distance the feeder must advance on every stroke. A long pitch combined with a high stroke rate demands higher speed and harder acceleration from the feeder.

At the quotation stage, provide these three together: **pitch length, number of stations and press strokes per minute.** The feeder is sized against that trio.

## 3. Sheet thickness and width

Thicker sheet requires higher grip force; wider sheet requires longer rolls. Servosteel servo feeders handle **0.4 – 4.0 mm** thickness and widths **up to 1,600 mm**, with 2 × Ø85 mm feed rolls.

Free feed speed is **35 m/min** — check that it matches your press rate.

## 4. Press synchronisation and pilot release

A feeder cannot work independently of the press. It must run in **full synchronisation** with the press cam signal and release the strip the moment the die's pilot pins engage it (pilot release). Without this function the pilots fight the strip and hole positions shift.

On Servosteel feeders the pilot release function is standard.

## 5. Do not skip the straightening question

Coil-set memory in strip coming off the coil causes direct problems in a progressive die: if the strip does not enter the die flat, station alignment breaks down. If your tolerances are tight, consider the feeder together with a straightener.

Instead of buying a separate machine, a [straightener servo feeder](/makineler/dogrultmali-servo-suruculer) can be chosen — straightening and feeding combine in one body, configured to sheet thickness with 7/9/11/13 roll options.

## 6. Recipe memory — the reality of multi-die shops

A shop rarely runs a single die. Re-entering pitch, speed and ramp values at every die change costs time and invites error.

Servosteel feeders store **250 recipes**; at a die change the operator recalls the recipe instead of repeating the setup.

## Why sourcing die and feeder together matters

A progressive die and its feeder are two interdependent parts: pitch tolerance, pilot release timing and strip layout only settle if they are designed together. When they come from different suppliers, responsibility blurs — the die maker points at the feeder and the feeder maker points at the die.
